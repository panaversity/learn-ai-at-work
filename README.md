**From absolute beginner to AI Worker manager.** Learn to delegate, verify and manage real work with Claude and ChatGPT. No coding required · 24 practical steps · PCAO-F and CCAO-F preparation.

> 🚧 **Under development.** Every step is written in full and is being tested with learners in Claude and ChatGPT. Expect small changes.

**Why a bootcamp?** Many learners have told us the PCAO-F exam is hard. Reading alone isn't enough: you forget what you don't practise. *You don't learn to drive by reading about driving. You learn by driving. The same applies to AI.* So in every step here you do real work with AI, check it, and keep it.

Welcome! **This is where your AI journey starts.** You don't need to know anything about AI, coding or GitHub to begin. If you can use email, a web browser and a spreadsheet, you are ready.

This bootcamp is for **knowledge workers**: people whose work is information, such as accountants, analysts, administrators, managers and assistants. Step by step, you learn to hand an AI a whole job, check what comes back, and then manage an **AI Worker**: an AI that holds a role and does part of a team's work every week, inside limits you set.

It is the hands-on companion to the book [The AI Agent Factory](https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/), from its first page to the end of Part II. That is everything you need for your first certification, **PCAO-F** (Panaversity Certified Associate: Foundations), and it prepares you for **CCAO-F** (Claude Certified Associate: Foundations), Anthropic's certification for professionals who use Claude at work.

## Start here: your first 15 minutes with AI

Before you install anything or learn any new words, try it. You need only a web browser.

1. Open [claude.ai](https://claude.ai) or [chatgpt.com](https://chatgpt.com) and sign in with a free account (ChatGPT also lets you try it without one).
2. Copy these meeting notes. They are made up, from Copperleaf Supply, the company you will work for in this bootcamp:

   ```text
   Copperleaf Supply, team meeting, Monday 2 November 2026. Present: Amira (owner), Rosa (finance manager), Ben (credit controller).
   - Harbourline Hotels still hasn't paid its July invoice. Ben will phone them by Wednesday 5 November.
   - Westgate College asked for a statement. Rosa will send it by the end of the week.
   - The new price list needs checking before it goes to customers.
   - The bank meeting is on Thursday 12 November. Ben will have the reminder list ready the day before.
   - Rosa will send the October report to the accountant on Friday.
   ```

3. Paste the notes into a new chat, then paste this below them and send:

   ```text
   Turn these meeting notes into an action plan: a table with each action, who owns it, and the deadline as a date. Then write a short follow-up email from Rosa to the team listing the actions. Don't invent anything that isn't in the notes; if something is missing or doesn't add up, say so.
   ```

4. **Now check it, line by line, against the notes.** This is the part that makes AI work trustworthy, and it is yours. Is every action there? Did it give the price-list check an owner that nobody named? What date did it give Ben's call? Look at a calendar: in 2026, 5 November is a Thursday, not a Wednesday, so the notes themselves contain a mistake. Did the AI notice, or quietly pick one?

That is the whole bootcamp in miniature: **hand over a job, then check what comes back.** Four ideas go with you from today:

1. **AI can be wrong even when it sounds sure.** Checking is part of the job, not an extra.
2. **AI doesn't know your organization's rules** unless you give them to it.
3. **Never put confidential or personal information into AI** unless your organization allows it. In this bootcamp, use only Copperleaf's made-up files.
4. **People stay responsible** for any decision that matters, and for anything that leaves the company.

Ready for more? Step 00 sets up the tools you will use from Step 01 on.

## What you will be able to do

By the end of this bootcamp you will be able to:

- hand Claude or ChatGPT a whole job (not just a question) and get back finished work: a sheet, a memo, slides;
- check AI work properly, tracing every number and claim to where it came from;
- explain in plain words why AI can sound right and still be wrong;
- write the documents a manager needs to delegate safely: a brief, a set of checks agreed in advance, and clear limits on what the AI may do;
- give an AI one official, up-to-date source of your company's rules;
- improve anything you write with the **Dr. Gilsdorf method**: review, grade, improve, repeat;
- set up work that runs without you, and fix it when it goes wrong;
- show a complete, checked piece of delegated work, and sit the PCAO-F practice exam with confidence.

On the way you pass three levels: **AI Work Practitioner**, **AI Worker Designer** and **AI Worker Manager** (see [Milestones and pacing](#milestones-and-pacing)).

**You learn by doing.** The book explains the ideas. This bootcamp is where you use them, on one company's real-looking work, in both Claude and ChatGPT. Every step ends with something you made, checked and kept.

## Your client: Copperleaf Supply

Every step is work for one client, so every step has a real reason behind it. **Copperleaf Supply** is a fictional, family-owned company that delivers coffee, cups, cleaning and kitchen supplies to cafés, restaurants and hotels. You join its finance team as the person who puts AI to work.

The work is **accounts receivable**: making sure customers pay what they owe. It is perfect practice, because it is full of numbers to check, rules to follow, and messages that must never go out unchecked.

- **Steps 1 to 12:** you use Claude and ChatGPT on Copperleaf's everyday work (matching payments to invoices, reporting who owes what, writing to late payers), and step back to see the bigger picture of where AI work is going.
- **Steps 13 to 23:** you describe, set up and manage one AI Worker, Copperleaf's **Receivables Worker**, which takes on part of that work every week.
- **Step 24:** you prove one complete job, from the brief to the checked result. You may use a task from your own job instead.

You work with three people: **Amira**, the owner; **Rosa**, the finance manager, your manager; and **Ben**, the credit controller, who chases late payments. Each step opens with **🍃 Copperleaf's ask**: what they need from you, and why.

Copperleaf's files (customer list, invoices, last week's payments, its credit policy and some customer emails) are in the [`copperleaf/`](copperleaf/) folder. They are all made up, so you can practise freely without risking anyone's real data.

**Not in finance?** The accounting is only the setting; the skills are the same in any job, and [Receivables in five minutes](copperleaf/README.md#receivables-in-five-minutes) explains the few money words you need. Copperleaf stays the one running project, but a few steps (1, 7, 12, 18 and 22) offer an optional *"In your own field"* exercise, using made-up material you write yourself, never real data. Changing the names in real material isn't enough, because other details can still identify people, so make it up from scratch:

| Teacher | Preparing a test and checking every answer key against the syllabus |

## How we work: local first, human in the loop

This bootcamp follows the **[Panaversity AI Method](docs/panaversity-ai-method.md)**, the way Panaversity's own team works with AI ([How the Panaversity team works](https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/claude-and-chatgpt-2/new-way-of-working/how-the-panaversity-team-works/#what-is-the-panaversity-ai-method) in the book). It has five steps: define the problem, set up the environment, write the four-part brief, improve the work with the Dr. Gilsdorf method, then check and commit. Each step of the bootcamp gives you the problem (Copperleaf's job) and the brief (its prompts); you learn to write your own in step 17. Items 1 to 3 below set up the environment, item 4 is how you improve the work, and item 5 is how you check and commit it. Claude does much of the work on your computer, and **you decide what is kept**.

1. **Your files live on your computer.** In Step 00 you make your own copy of the bootcamp on GitHub (your *fork*) and download it to your computer with GitHub Desktop (your *clone*).
2. **You work in a Claude project.** You create a project in the Claude app, add your bootcamp folder and your GitHub fork to it, and give it one standing instruction: *never commit or push; I do that myself*. A project has a coordinator you talk to in its main chat, threads that do the work, and settings for its instructions ([Projects redesigned](https://claude.com/resources/articles/projects-redesigned), Anthropic).
3. **One thread per step.** Claude reads Copperleaf's files in your folder and saves what it makes into the step's `work/` folder. It asks before it changes anything.
4. **You improve it in rounds: the Dr. Gilsdorf method.** For anything you write, you set a goal (9.5 out of 10) and a few pass-or-fail checks, such as "every figure matches Copperleaf's files". You ask the best AI you have to run the checks, grade the work out of 10 with evidence, improve it in a small step, and repeat. Then you give it to a different AI for an independent review, without showing it the first AI's grades: best by opening the same folder in Codex (review only), or else as a zip that Claude makes, given to ChatGPT on the web. You bring that review back; Claude weighs each point and runs the checks again. A failed check always counts for more than a grade. Zia Khan, who leads this bootcamp, named it after Dr. Gilsdorf, a business communication professor at Arizona State University who taught that you improve writing by going over it many times. The book explains it in [How the Panaversity team works](https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/claude-and-chatgpt-2/new-way-of-working/how-the-panaversity-team-works/#how-do-we-improve-the-work). You first use it in step 2. A grade is an opinion, so you still check the facts yourself.
5. **You check, then you keep.** You read what changed in GitHub Desktop, check the facts against their sources, open any Word or slide file the job made, and only then commit it and push it to GitHub. Keeping your work as plain text (Markdown and CSV) where you can makes every change show line by line. Nothing reaches GitHub until a person has read it. That is the *human in the loop*, and the book explains why it matters in [Appendix: versions and pull requests](https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/appendices/claude-and-chatgpt-2/appendix-versions-and-pull-requests/).
6. **You repeat the job in ChatGPT.** ChatGPT doesn't work on your folder, so you attach the files the job needs (or a zip of them), give it the same job, save its result into `work/` too, and compare the two.

> **Good to know.** Claude's projects are new and still rolling out, so menu names may change; this bootcamp describes them in general terms. Threads work directly on the folder on your computer. If you don't have projects yet, use the **Code** tab of Claude Desktop on your bootcamp folder instead ([Claude 2.0: the Code tab and running work](https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/claude-and-chatgpt-2/tour/claude-code-tab/)). Everything else stays the same.

> 💡 **Keep your computer awake.** When Claude works in your folder, it works on your computer, so a long job or a scheduled task stops if the computer goes to sleep. In Claude Desktop, open **Settings → Desktop app** and turn on **Keep computer awake**. Closing a laptop's lid still puts it to sleep, so keep the lid open for long jobs. For a job that must run while your computer is off, you can start a cloud session on your GitHub fork instead, and check what it brings back before you keep it.

> ⚠️ **Your fork is public.** Anyone can see it. Never put real personal, customer or company data in it, or into the AI apps during the bootcamp. Use Copperleaf's made-up files, or material from your own field that you make up from scratch (changing the names in real material isn't enough) ([10.2 Can this data go in?](https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/managing-ai-workers/governance-and-responsible-use/can-this-data-go-in/)).

> ⛔ **Don't send changes back to this repository.** Your work stays in your own fork, and you submit it through the submission form. Never open a pull request to the bootcamp's repo.

The whole bootcamp takes about 130 to 170 hours: for example, about 10 hours a week for four months. At the end of milestones 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 (steps 7, 16, 20, 23 and 24) you record a short **project defence**: a few minutes of you explaining your own work, in your own words ([SUBMITTING.md](SUBMITTING.md#10-defend-your-work)). It is how you show that you, not just the AI, understand the work.

**Three levels along the way.** The milestones group into three levels, so you have a complete, useful capability long before the end. They are this bootcamp's own markers of progress, not extra certifications:

| Level | Steps | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI Work Practitioner | 1 to 12 | Use AI to complete and check professional work, and explain why it can be wrong |
| AI Worker Designer | 13 to 20 | Define, set up and govern an AI Worker |
| AI Worker Manager | 21 to 24 | Manage recurring work, diagnose failures and prove outcomes |

**Less help as you go.** The prompts thin out on purpose, because copying a good prompt is not the same as being able to delegate:

| Steps | Your prompts |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 12 | **Follow:** the step gives you every prompt. Read each one before you paste it |
| 13 to 20 | **Modify:** the step gives you the prompts, and you adapt them to what you found. Each step's `NOTES.md` records one prompt you changed, and why |
| 21 to 23 | **Construct:** the step says what each prompt must ask for. You write it, then compare it with an example kept folded away |
| 24 | **On your own:** only the task and its checks. You define, run and verify the job yourself |

**Checking runs through the whole bootcamp.** From step 1, everything is checked before it is kept. From step 18, the checks are written down before the work even starts. Every step's checklist asks you to trace its numbers and claims to their sources.

### How each step works

1. **Read first.** Each step starts with a short **Learn first** list of book lessons. Read them before you begin. It ends with the book's **Check yourself** page for that section: do it when you finish the step, and go back to any domain where you score low.
2. **Start a new thread** for the step in your Claude project, and read Copperleaf's ask.
3. **Do the job with Claude**, pasting the step's prompts one at a time. Read what Claude plans before you let it start.
4. **Check the result** before you keep it, using the step's checks.
5. **Repeat it in ChatGPT** (or write the transfer plan) and compare the two.
6. **Keep it:** read the changes in GitHub Desktop, then commit, tag and push them yourself. Claude never does this for you.

**The book's labs.** Most steps link a book lab as "the book's version of this step". Read it to see the method, then do the Copperleaf version: it replaces the book's lab, so you don't do the same job twice. Do the book's lab as well only if you want extra practice; the times above don't include it.

Stuck? Ask Claude in your thread to explain the step in simpler words. It is a good teacher, and asking is part of learning.

## What comes next

When you finish this bootcamp and pass PCAO-F, you have a choice:

- **Use it in your work.** Many people stop here, as confident managers of AI work. That is the book's "everyday professional" route.
- **Prove you can manage an AI Worker.** The book's *Managing the AI Worker* exam is graded on five pieces of real work, and you will already have made all five here: a Four-Part Brief (step 17), a review contract written before you delegate (step 18), an Authority Envelope (step 19), your review of a run you didn't watch (step 18) and your diagnosis of a failed run (step 23). See the [Certification and Portfolio Roadmaps](https://agentfactory.panaversity.org/certification-and-portfolio-roadmaps/).
- **Learn to build.** If you want to build AI products for clients, continue with **[Build and Sell MCP Apps: The CCDV-F Bootcamp Part I](https://github.com/panaversity/learn-mcp)**, which teaches you to build the connectors and apps that AI Workers use. Much carries straight over: GitHub Desktop, working in a Claude project, and checking work before you keep it. Three things are new, and Part I expects them from its first step:
  - **the terminal**: typing commands instead of clicking buttons;
  - **reading code**: Part I is written in TypeScript, and you need to follow it, not write it from scratch;
  - **data and the web**: how a web request and JSON work, and simple SQL queries on a database.

  What you learn here is exactly what you will build there:

  | Here you learn | In the MCP bootcamp you build |
  | --- | --- |
  | The Authority Envelope | Authentication and authorization |
  | Approved company knowledge (the KSoR) | Resources and governed search |
  | Company-specific rules | Skills for each company (tenant) |
  | Checking output | Automated tests and evals |
  | A person approves before anything leaves | Confirmations before an action runs |
  | Recurring work | Tasks, events and durable workflows |
  | Reading a failed run's record | Logs, traces and troubleshooting |
  | The business need behind each job | Tool and connector design |

  Instructions, permissions and checks (Step 03) are the same idea one level down: there, you enforce the permissions in code.

  Prepare with chapters 12 to 15 of the book, then take Part I's own [Step 00 readiness check](https://github.com/panaversity/learn-mcp/tree/main/step-00-readiness-check), which tests each of these and tells you what to read for any gap. After Part I comes **Build and Sell Self Improving AI Workers: The CCDV-F Bootcamp Part II**, which is under development.

**Don't just study AI. Put AI to work.**
